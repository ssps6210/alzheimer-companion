# Content Batching and Repurposing for a Solo Creator with 3–5 Hours a Week (2025–2026)

> Method note: These notes come from about 15 web searches done on 2026-10-28. buffer.com could not be fetched in this session (DNS blocked by the session's network allowlist). Buffer figures therefore come from search-result summaries of Buffer's pages and from secondary coverage (Social Media Today and others), not from the full articles. Most frequency and batching numbers come from tool vendors (Buffer, Hootsuite, SocialKit, AdaptlyPost, Metricool), which sell scheduling software. Read them as marketing-adjacent data, not neutral research.

## Q1. Documented batching systems and time per step

### Takeaway
The documented systems share one pattern. A single long-form "hub" or "pillar" piece is made each week or month and then cut into many short "spokes." Batching itself means grouping the steps: plan, then write, then produce, then schedule. Published time benchmarks for a solo operator cluster at about 3–4 hours per week for multi-channel output. These figures come from vendors and self-reports. I found no independent study of them.

### Cited Findings
- **Gary Vaynerchuk "reverse pyramid":** content starts from one long-form pillar piece (a vlog, keynote or Q&A show). His team then cuts it into dozens of smaller pieces fitted to each platform. A podcast can serve as the pillar for someone who is not comfortable on video. Source is his own blog post from 2019, so it is older than the target window. — [GaryVaynerchuk.com](https://garyvaynerchuk.com/THE-GARYVEE-CONTENT-STRATEGY-HOW-TO-GROW-AND-DISTRIBUTE-YOUR-BRANDS-SOCIAL-MEDIA-CONTENT/)
- Third-party summary of the GaryVee loop: document the pillar, repurpose it into micro-content, distribute, listen, apply what you learned, repeat. Distribution reportedly happens in two passes. The first pass drives viewers to the pillar and the second pushes the micro-content. — [Pepper Content](https://peppercontent.io/blog/garyvee-content-strategy); [Avidly](https://avidlyagency.com/blog/watch-what-i-do-not-what-i-say-deconstructing-what-gary-vee-does)
- **Justin Welsh Content OS:** the official course page calls it a multi-step system for producing a newsletter plus 6–12 social posts every week. It gives no time estimate. (This is a paid product, so its claims are marketing.) — [learn.justinwelsh.me](https://learn.justinwelsh.me/content)
- A third-party summary of Content OS 2.0 describes one long-form hub (his Saturday newsletter) that yields 6–10 short-form spokes, extracted through a 9-step pipeline. — [summary vault, 2026-04](https://hq-raydata-co.pages.dev/vault/06-reference/2026-04-06-content-os-welsh/)
- Welsh reportedly said in an X AMA that he tags high performers in a spreadsheet and republishes them 6 and 12 months later, and that after a few years he is "mostly playing the hits." This is a secondhand report from a vendor blog. — [Tweet Hunter](https://tweethunter.io/blog/justin-welsh-content-repurposing-system)
- Reports of Welsh's hours conflict. One write-up says he works about 10 hours a week using SOPs, Airtable and automations. Another piece from the same publisher says about 2 hours a day. Neither figure is confirmed by Welsh. — [The Mango Network](https://themangonetwork.substack.com/p/the-creator-operating-system); [The Mango Network repurpose playbook](https://themangonetwork.substack.com/p/the-repurpose-playbook-maximize-your)
- **Time per step (SocialKit, 2026, vendor):** for a solo operator making about 10 posts, planning takes 45–60 minutes and writing everything in one block takes 1–2 hours. — [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/batch-content-creation-workflow)
- One vendor (AdaptlyPost, 2025) claims a single 3-hour session produces 30 posts and saves 25+ hours a month compared with posting daily. This is an unverified marketing claim. — [AdaptlyPost](https://adaptlypost.com/blog/content-batching)
- A multi-channel creator reports spending about 4 hours a week on content, from idea to scheduling. She advises beginners to batch one week ahead before trying longer runs (2024, self-report). — [GoTranscript](https://gotranscript.com/public/CHANGE%20DUPLICATED%20SLUG%2086584554)
- Averi (January 2026, vendor) assumes a solo marketer has maybe 5–10 hours a week for content. It proposes a monthly sequence: plan, create long-form, create short-form, then produce and schedule. — [Averi](https://www.averi.ai/how-to/content-creation-when-you-re-the-only-marketer-the-batching-method)

### Inferences
- A workable 4-hour weekly block for this creator, assembled from the step benchmarks above (an estimate, not a sourced figure):
  - 0:30 for planning, done monthly and amortised: one theme per month mapped to the five pillars.
  - 1:00 to write one hub piece, for example a dev log or one caregiver story of 600–1,000 words in zh-TW plus an EN summary.
  - 1:00 to cut it into atoms: Threads text in two languages, a 5–8 slide carousel script, and a 30–45 second Short script.
  - 0:45 to record or design, with one talking-head take or screen recording and a reusable carousel template.
  - 0:30 to localise and schedule (zh-TW to zh-CN conversion with rewriting, platform-specific captions).
  - Engagement then runs separately at 10–15 minutes a day (see Q5). The total lands at about 4–5.5 hours.
- Welsh's "hub to 6–10 spokes" ratio fits a 3–5 hour budget better than the GaryVee model. GaryVee's model assumes a video team.
- Build-in-public dev logs are natural hubs because the raw material (commits, issues, user feedback) already exists.

### Gaps
- I found no independent (non-vendor) time-and-motion study of batching for solo creators.
- I could not fetch Buffer's or Hootsuite's own batching guides because the domain was blocked or did not come up in search.

## Q2. Repurposing pipelines and what each platform needs adapted

### Takeaway
Repurposing has to change the content each time, not copy it. Meta now actively demotes duplicated or unoriginal content: Instagram does it platform-wide as of April 2026, and Facebook since July 2025. The platforms reward different native formats: Threads favours conversational text, Instagram favours shareable carousels and Reels, Reddit punishes promotion, and Xiaohongshu brings tight health-content regulation plus link restrictions. The adapted content should carry new framing, native formatting and no cross-platform watermarks.

### Cited Findings
- **Instagram originality:** in 2022 Mosseri announced ranking that boosts original content and demotes Reels with third-party watermarks such as TikTok's. Originality is predicted from signals such as whether Instagram has seen the content before. — [TubeFilter 2022](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/04/21/instagram-adam-mosseri-algorithm-change-original-content/) (older than the target window)
- In April 2026 the originality policy was extended from Reels to the whole platform. Accounts that mostly share uncredited or unmodified content are not recommended to non-followers. The suggested options are the built-in repost tool, collaborating, or adding substantial creative input. — [PetaPixel, 2026-04-30](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- **Facebook (July 14, 2025):** Meta penalises accounts that repeatedly reuse others' work. Duplicate posts get reduced distribution, and repeat offenders can lose monetisation. Commentary or added perspective still counts as legitimate. — [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/07/223-meta-cracks-down-on-unoriginal-facebook-content/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/16/facebook-to-fight-unoriginal-content/)
  - Note: this policy targets reuse of *other people's* work. Cross-posting your own original content is not the target, but the duplicate-detection system could still treat identical uploads conservatively. That last point is my inference.
- **Threads (Meta's 2025 educational hub, via secondary coverage):** the best performers post original content tailored to Threads. Media posts that include text outperform those without. Replies account for nearly half of views. Posts that spark discussion are more likely to be recommended. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-how-to-grow-your-threads-presence/730488/); [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/meta-introduces-educational-resources-for-threads-to-help-creators-grow-on-the-presence)
- The head of Threads, Emily Dalton Smith, describes Threads as "a real-time conversation app" where "most of the content will be distributed in just a few hours over the course of the day." — [Business Insider India](https://businessinsider.in/tech/news/metas-head-of-threads-shares-advice-about-how-and-when-to-post-as-the-debate-swirls-around-engagement-bait/amp_articleshow/114227410.cms) (2024)
- **Instagram signals (Mosseri, 2025, via secondary sources):** the ranking signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach. Sends matter more for reaching non-followers. Mosseri has recommended carousels for reach since fall 2024. Trial Reels are shown only to non-followers and should be compared only with other Trial Reels. Claims that "DM shares are worth 3–5x likes" are unverified. — [Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm/); [Contentdrips 2026](https://contentdrips.com/blog/2026/06/instagram-reels-vs-carousels-2026-guide/)
- A Metricool/HypeAuditor study of 700 million posts (January–June 2025) found that for accounts under 1,000 followers, Reels have a median of 134 views, against 56 for carousels and 23 for images. Carousels pull ahead for larger accounts. — reported via [Contentdrips](https://contentdrips.com/blog/2026/06/instagram-reels-vs-carousels-2026-guide/) (vendor data, secondhand)
- **Reddit:** Content Policy Rule 2 bans spam and manipulation. Reddiquette's spirit is to be "a redditor with a website, not a website with an account." The widely cited "10% / 9:1" self-promotion ratio is a community convention and not an official Reddit rule. Individual subreddits are often far stricter. — [Redship guide 2026](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules); [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/business/how-to-promote-your-website-or-business-on-reddit-without-being-flagged-as-spam)
- **Xiaohongshu format (marketing blogs, not official):** 3:4 cover images, direct titles of 20 Chinese characters or fewer, and 3–10 hashtags at the end of the note. — [WalkTheChat](https://walkthechat.com/tips-optimize-little-red-book-xiaohongshu-content-marketing/); [Hicom Asia](https://www.hicom-asia.com/how-do-we-create-a-post-in-xiaohongshu/)
- **Xiaohongshu links:** sources conflict. One says the platform blocks third-party external links entirely, including mentions of competing apps. Another says profile links are disallowed but links in notes and comments are possible at some risk. Neither source is official. — [ScribeCount](https://scribecount.com/author-resource/social-media-marketing-for-authors/xiaohongshu-red-for-indie-authors); [Shoplazza](https://www.shoplazza.com/blog/leveraging-tiktok-refugee-trend-for-content-creators?hs_amp=true)
- **This is a major risk for the dementia pillar.** In August 2025, Chinese regulators (CAC, NHC, SAMR, TCM admin) issued a joint notice. Platforms must verify the medical credentials of health-content creators. Newly registered accounts are barred from posting health-related content until certified. Health education content must cite sources and disclose AI-generated material. Contact details and purchase links in medical or health ads are prohibited. — [People's Daily EN](https://en.people.cn/n3/2025/0804/c90000-20348885.html); [China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202508/04/WS688ff13aa310c0209d01abff.html); [ECNS](https://www.ecns.cn/news/society/2025-08-04/detail-ihetutvf4242412.shtml)
- Marketing blogs say Xiaohongshu restricts health-claim terms, using AI plus human review, and updates its term lists without public notice. These claims are unverified. — [Hashmeta](https://hashmeta.com/blog/xiaohongshu-restricted-categories-what-singapore-healthcare-and-finance-brands-cant-say/)

### Inferences
- **Suggested pipeline from one hub** (a dev log or a caregiver story), with rough adaptation times (my estimates):
  1. Hub: a GitHub/blog dev log or story in zh-TW, with an EN version. About 60 minutes.
  2. Threads: 1–3 short text posts ending in an open question, posted separately in zh-TW and EN, with any link in a reply. About 10 minutes.
  3. Instagram carousel: 5–8 slides of "tips" or "what I learned" with text on the image, built from a Canva/Figma template. Optimised for saves and sends. About 25 minutes.
  4. Reel / YouTube Short: 30–45 seconds of talking head or app screen recording with burned-in captions. Export a clean file without any watermark and upload it natively to each platform. About 30 minutes.
  5. Facebook Page and groups: a longer narrative caption with a personal story frame. In caregiver groups, ask rather than promote. About 10 minutes.
  6. Reddit: a text-first post that delivers value (lessons, an open-source repo, asking for feedback) and follows each subreddit's rules, posted only in communities where you already participate. About 15 minutes.
  7. Xiaohongshu: rewrite in Simplified Chinese with mainland vocabulary rather than character conversion alone (for example 视频 instead of 影片, 软件 instead of 軟體). Use a 3:4 cover and a title of 20 characters or fewer, with no external link. Keep it framed as a caregiver's personal experience or an app dev log, not medical or health advice, because of the August 2025 rules. About 20 minutes.
- **Language:** keep zh-TW (Taiwan) as the source. HK readers accept Traditional Chinese, though Cantonese idioms help. zh-CN for Xiaohongshu needs vocabulary localisation, not just OpenCC conversion.
- **Links:** Threads, Instagram and Xiaohongshu deprioritise or block links in the post body. Put links in replies, the bio or the profile. Reddit and Facebook groups tolerate links when the post is clearly non-commercial and allowed by group rules.
- The "privacy/safety transparency" and "dev log" pillars carry the least regulatory risk on Xiaohongshu. "Dementia communication tips" is the pillar most likely to be classed as health content there.

### Gaps
- I found no official Xiaohongshu documentation on link policy or on how the 2025 health rules apply to caregiver-experience content, or whether they apply to overseas or Taiwan accounts.
- I found no official YouTube statement on whether identical Shorts and Reels uploads are penalised. Watermark demotion is documented only for Instagram.

## Q3. Posting frequency and results for small or new accounts

### Takeaway
The evidence consistently says consistency beats volume. Instagram has a measured sweet spot of 3–5 posts a week. Meta's Threads guidance is 2–5 posts a week. Hootsuite's higher figures (Facebook 1–2 a day, Threads 2–3 a day) describe business accounts with staff, not solo creators. No official YouTube Shorts frequency exists, and no Xiaohongshu frequency data was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Instagram (Buffer, August 2025, 2M+ posts from 100k+ accounts):** 3–5 posts a week is "likely the sweet spot" for reach and growth. The biggest jump in reach per post comes from moving from 1–2 to 3–5 posts a week, about a 12% lift. Returns diminish beyond that. 6–9 or 10+ posts a week is suggested only for full-time or IG-focused creators. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/) (vendor; not fetched in full)
- **Mosseri (Instagram):** there is no fixed frequency. For some people "five times a day works great," and for others "once a month" does. He would rather a creator post twice a week for two years than every day for two months and then quit. He has also said that in general, posting more brings more reach. He has recommended about 2 Stories a day. — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/how-often-you-should-post-on-instagram/); [techissuestoday](https://techissuestoday.com/adam-mosseri-instagram-posting-schedule-advice); Stories figure via [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/) (secondary reports of Mosseri's videos and broadcast channel)
- **Threads (Meta's 2025 educational hub, via secondary coverage):** 2–5 posts a week is recommended for audience growth, with weekend posts getting higher engagement. Another report cites a minimum of about twice a week. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-how-to-grow-your-threads-presence/730488/); [Social Samosa](https://socialsamosa.com/news-2/meta-threads-tips-mini-site-boost-user-engagement-7345926)
- **Hootsuite 2025 (vendor, partly "theoretical"):** Instagram 3–5 posts a week (at least 3), Facebook 1–2 a day, Threads 2–3 a day. Average business accounts posted on Instagram 9.3 times a week in Q1 2025. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/)
- **Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026 (52M+ posts, 10 platforms, March 2026):** accounts that skipped posting in a given week grew followers more slowly than their own baseline. Higher volume brings more total engagement and follower growth, but reach per post falls as frequency rises. Coverage notes that timing and frequency act as amplifiers, while relevance and interaction matter more. — [Buffer report](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement/); [SocialEcho summary](https://www.socialecho.net/en/blog/docs/2026-social-media-engagement-report-52m-posts-insight)
- **Buffer consistency study (26 weeks, 100k+ users):** creators who posted in 20 or more of the 26 weeks got about 450% more engagement per post than those who posted in 4 weeks or fewer. — via [Buffer creator growth playbook](https://buffer.com/resources/creator-growth-playbook) (search-result summary, not fetched)
- **Threads engagement trend:** Buffer's median Threads engagement rate fell from 4.76% to 3.60% between January 2024 and January 2025. — [Buffer data](https://buffer.com/resources/buffer-data/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-linkedin-and-threads-engagement-declined-in-2025/814141/)
- **YouTube Shorts:** I found no official numeric guidance. Third-party blogs range from daily posting for small channels to 1–3 Shorts a week. Claims of 1.8–2.4x faster subscriber growth from "internal testing" have no primary source. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/how-often-to-post-youtube-shorts); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/how-often-should-you-post-shorts-to-grow-faster)
- **Small IG accounts:** Reels get far more reach than carousels or images for accounts under 1,000 followers (median views 134 vs 56 vs 23; Metricool/HypeAuditor, H1 2025). — via [Contentdrips](https://contentdrips.com/blog/2026/06/instagram-reels-vs-carousels-2026-guide/)

### Inferences
- **Minimum viable cadence for a 3–5 hour budget** (an inference that combines the sources above):
  - Threads: 3 a week per language. This is within Meta's 2–5 range and is cheap to produce because it is text.
  - Instagram: 2–3 a week (1 Reel, 1 carousel, an optional third piece) plus a few Stories. This is below Buffer's sweet spot, but Mosseri explicitly favours a sustainable rate.
  - YouTube Shorts: 1–2 a week, reusing the Reel's raw file without any watermark.
  - Facebook Page: 2–3 a week, which is far below Hootsuite's business figure. Groups are better for caregiver reach than Pages.
  - Reddit: 1–2 value posts a month plus regular comments.
  - Xiaohongshu: 1–2 notes a week.
- The strongest documented effect is not skipping weeks entirely (Buffer's 2026 report and the 26-week study). A smaller cadence that never breaks beats a burst-and-quit pattern.

### Gaps
- I found no official YouTube or Facebook Page frequency guidance from 2025–2026.
- I found no reliable data on Xiaohongshu posting frequency in English or Chinese.
- Buffer's datasets skew toward Buffer users (often businesses) and do not isolate brand-new accounts under 1,000 followers. The Metricool reach figure is the only small-account cut I found.

## Q4. Templates, evergreen libraries, recycling top performers and calendars

### Takeaway
"Playing the hits" means logging top performers and re-publishing reworked versions 6–12 months later. Together with reusable visual templates and a monthly theme calendar, it is the main lever documented for a time-poor solo creator.

### Cited Findings
- Welsh keeps a spreadsheet of high performers and repurposes them at 6 and 12 months. — [Tweet Hunter](https://tweethunter.io/blog/justin-welsh-content-repurposing-system)
- Averi's monthly sequence (plan, then long-form, then short-form, then production and scheduling) works as a monthly calendar structure. — [Averi](https://www.averi.ai/how-to/content-creation-when-you-re-the-only-marketer-the-batching-method)
- Beginners are advised to batch one week ahead before trying a month. — [GoTranscript](https://gotranscript.com/public/CHANGE%20DUPLICATED%20SLUG%2086584554)
- Meta's originality rules mean recycled posts need substantial changes (new hook, new format, added perspective) rather than verbatim reposts. — [PetaPixel 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)

### Inferences
- Suggested library: a simple sheet (Notion, Airtable or GitHub Projects) with columns for pillar, hub link, each platform variant, language, date, views/saves/sends/replies, an "evergreen?" flag and a "reuse after" date.
- Evergreen candidates: the origin story, the top 10 dementia communication tips (each tip can become a separate atom), the privacy FAQ, and "how the voice companion works" explainers. Dev logs are time-bound but can be summarised quarterly.
- Templates to build once: a carousel template in each language, a Short intro and caption style, a Threads question template, a Reddit post skeleton (context, what I built, what I learned, ask), and a Xiaohongshu cover template at 3:4.
- Monthly theme mapping example: Month A covers communication tips plus a dev log, Month B covers privacy plus community co-creation. The origin story is resurfaced quarterly.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 quantitative study of how recycled posts perform compared with new ones.

## Q5. Keeping up engagement in 10–15 minutes a day

### Takeaway
Replying is the highest-leverage cheap activity, especially on Threads. Buffer reports engagement lifts from replying to comments of +42% on Threads, +21% on Instagram and +9% on Facebook, though the data is correlational. Meta says replies make up nearly half of Threads views.

### Cited Findings
- Buffer (November 2025, about 2M posts from 220k+ accounts): replying to comments correlates with engagement lifts of Threads +42%, LinkedIn +30%, Instagram +21%, Facebook +9%, X +8% and Bluesky +5%. Buffer cautions that the result may be reverse-causal, because popular posts attract more replies. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/replying-to-comments-boosts-engagement/); [Buffer Threads](https://buffer.com/resources/threads-comments-engagement/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/report-looks-at-post-replies-and-how-they-impact-engagement-rates/805370/)
- Meta says replies account for nearly half of Threads views, and posts that spark conversation are more likely to be recommended. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-how-to-grow-your-threads-presence/730488/)
- Threads distribution happens within a few hours of posting. — [Business Insider India](https://businessinsider.in/tech/news/metas-head-of-threads-shares-advice-about-how-and-when-to-post-as-the-debate-swirls-around-engagement-bait/amp_articleshow/114227410.cms)

### Inferences
- **A 15-minute daily routine** (my construction):
  - 5 minutes: reply to every comment on posts from the last 24 hours, Threads and IG first. Schedule Threads posts for a time you can check within 1–3 hours, such as a lunch break.
  - 5 minutes: leave 3–5 substantive replies in other caregiver or dev conversations (Threads topics, FB groups, relevant subreddits). This also builds Reddit's non-promotional activity ratio.
  - 5 minutes: save good questions and comments into the idea bank, which becomes the community co-creation pillar.
- Weekly, spend 15 minutes in the batch session on Xiaohongshu, Reddit and YouTube comments rather than checking them daily.
- Use Meta Business Suite's unified inbox for IG and FB to cut app switching. This is a general practice and not sourced here.

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring the best time-boxed engagement routine. The routine above is synthesis.

## Q6. Avoiding burnout as a solo creator with a day job

### Takeaway
Burnout is common: 52–62% of creators report it in 2025 surveys. Platform leadership (Mosseri) explicitly endorses a lower but sustainable cadence over intense bursts. The design principles are a fixed weekly time box, a minimum cadence that never breaks, and a backlog of evergreen posts.

### Cited Findings
- Creators 4 Mental Health (2025, 542 full- and part-time North American creators): 62% report burnout, 69% obsess over content performance, and 89% lack access to specialised mental-health resources. — [TubeFilter, 2025-11-12](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/11/12/creators-4-mental-health-burnout-study-results/amp/)
- Billion Dollar Boy / Censuswide (July 2025, 1,000 US and UK creators): 52% have experienced burnout and 37% are considering quitting. — [Billion Dollar Boy](https://www.billiondollarboy.com/news/over-half-of-creators-face-burnout); [CosmeticsDesign Europe](https://www.cosmeticsdesign-europe.com/Article/2025/07/17/52-of-content-creators-say-they-have-experienced-burnout)
- SideHustles.com (2025, side hustlers generally, not creators specifically): 67% are burned out, and nearly 1 in 5 say the side hustle drains them more than their main job. — via [SelfEmployed](https://www.selfemployed.com/?p=103595) (secondary)
- Mosseri: twice a week for two years beats daily posting for two months followed by quitting. — [techissuestoday](https://techissuestoday.com/adam-mosseri-instagram-posting-schedule-advice)
- Buffer 2026: frequency and timing act as amplifiers, while relevance and interaction matter more. — [SocialEcho summary](https://www.socialecho.net/en/blog/docs/2026-social-media-engagement-report-52m-posts-insight)

### Inferences
- Guardrails for this creator:
  - Run one fixed batch session a week (for example 2.5–3 hours on Saturday morning) plus 15 minutes a day on weekdays, with a hard cap of 5 hours a week.
  - Keep a buffer of 2–3 weeks of evergreen posts for crunch weeks at the day job.
  - Drop or pause the lowest-ROI platform first, likely FB Page or Xiaohongshu given the regulatory friction, rather than skipping weeks everywhere.
  - Check analytics monthly, not daily, given the 69% "obsess over performance" finding.
  - Use the open-source community (contributors, caregivers) as content sources to share the load.
- Dev logs double as project documentation, so the work has value even when a post underperforms.

### Gaps
- I found no study specific to creators with full-time jobs. The SideHustles.com data covers side hustlers generally.
- I did not verify the Patreon State of Create 2025 figure ("78% say burnout affects motivation"), which appeared only in a secondary roundup.
